# Scientific plotting style

- Use a white background, dark text and quiet grid lines.
- Label axes with variable meaning and unit; keep transformations and normalization explicit.
- Use one restrained color root for a single series and at most a few distinct roots when category identity matters.
- Never rely on color alone: combine line style, marker, fill, order, faceting or direct labels.
- Avoid default red/green semantics, gradients, decorative backgrounds and 3D perspective.
- Prefer direct labels when they reduce legend lookup; otherwise place a compact legend where it does not cover evidence.
- Keep comparable panels on comparable scales unless a clearly labeled focused scale is essential.
- Use readable CJK/Latin font fallbacks; verify minus signs, subscripts and symbols in the exported file.
- Save PNG at publication resolution and PDF/SVG for vectors where supported.
- Put the supported interpretation in the caption or surrounding prose. Keep the chart title descriptive rather than duplicating the caption.

Inspect the final output at normal reading size and in grayscale. Fix clipped labels, excessive decimals, tiny type, detached annotations and inconsistent units before delivery.
